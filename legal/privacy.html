Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 September 2026

TraxJourney turns your activities, transport legs, photos and notes into one journey you can keep, share and export. This policy explains what the hosted service at traxjourney.com collects, why, who else receives it, how long it is kept and how you can get it back or delete it.

TraxJourney is free software. Someone else running their own copy of it (a "self-hosted" server) is responsible for that server and its own policy; this page only covers the service we run.

1. What we collect

Your account

  • Email and password sign-up: your email address, your first and last name (stored together as your display name) and your password, which we store only as a salted bcrypt hash. We send you an email to confirm the address; the confirmation link expires after 24 hours.
  • Sign in with Google: the identifier Google gives your account, your email address, your name and the address of your Google profile picture. We never receive your Google password.
  • When your account was created, whether your email is confirmed, your plan, and whether you have turned on encryption.

Your journeys

  • Trips: names, dates, settings, day notes (difficulty, where you slept, weather), counters and the order of everything in a trip.
  • Activities and routes: GPS tracks, start and end points, times, distances, elevation, speed and, when your source provides them, heart-rate averages and maximums, whether you uploaded them as GPX files or imported them.
  • Transport legs (train, flight, bus, boat): start and end places, dates and, for trains, the train number, together with the route we work out for them.
  • Memories and journal entries: titles, text, date, time, location and photos. Journal entries are private: in a shared trip each person sees only their own.
  • Photos: we keep the file you upload exactly as it is, including any embedded metadata such as the camera, the time and the GPS position where it was taken, plus a smaller preview we generate from it (the preview carries no such metadata).
  • Comments and likes you leave on memories, with the display name you had at the time.
  • Posters you generate, and the settings used to make them.

People you met

You can record people and groups you met on a trip: their name, email, phone number, Polarsteps profile, social media handles, nationalities, where they live, notes and a photo. This is information about other people. Only add what they would be comfortable with. These entries are visible to you and to the companions of that trip, and are never included in a public share link.

Travel companions

When you invite someone to a trip by email, we store their email address with the invitation and send them one email with a link to join. Companions of a trip can see each other's display name and profile picture.

Connected services (only if you connect them)

  • Strava: the access and refresh tokens Strava issues when you authorise us (read access to your activities, including private ones), a cached copy of your activity list, and the details and GPS streams of the activities you add to a trip.
  • Polarsteps: the remember_token sign-in cookie you paste in, your Polarsteps user id and username, and the trips, steps and photos you choose to import. The cookie gives access to your Polarsteps account, so disconnect it when you no longer need it.
  • Immich: the address of your Immich server and the API key you enter. Photos you browse from Immich pass through our server to reach you but are not stored, unless you add one to a memory.

Disconnecting a service in Settings deletes its stored token or key.

Payments

If you buy a paid plan, payment is handled by Stripe. We send Stripe your email address, your TraxJourney account ID and the plan you chose, and we store the Stripe customer and subscription identifiers, your plan, its status, when the current period ends and any change you have scheduled. Your card details are entered on Stripe's own pages and never reach our servers.

Usage of storage and share links

  • How much storage your account uses, to apply plan limits.
  • When someone opens one of your share links: if they are signed in, which account opened it and when (you can see their display name and email address in the trip's visitor list); if not, a random identifier their browser or app generated, so we can count distinct visitors.

Technical data

  • Server logs: each request produces a log line with the time, the address of the page or API route requested (including any parameters in the address), the result, how long it took, your account ID if you were signed in, and the network address the request came from. Errors are logged with the details needed to fix them.
  • Metrics: aggregate counters such as the number of requests, sign-ins, registrations and app launches. They are totals, not linked to your account or device.
  • Your device's location is used only on your device, when you ask the map to show where you are or to fill in a location. It is sent to us only if you then save that location in a memory, journal entry or encounter.

3. Encryption

You can turn on end-to-end encryption in Settings. Your device then encrypts the following before sending them, with keys we never receive:

  • memory titles and text;
  • journal entry text;
  • the names, GPS tracks, start and end points and elevation profiles of the activities in your own trips.

Everything else is not end-to-end encrypted, and we can technically read it: trip names, dates and times, the locations of memories, journal entries and encounters, photos, transport legs, people and encounters, comments, the cached activity list from Strava, and all account, payment and technical data. Anything you publish through a share link is readable by whoever has the link. All traffic between the app and our servers is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).

4. What becomes public when you share

A share link lets anyone who has it view the trip without signing in. It shows the trip's name, your display name, its routes, transport legs, dates and statistics and, unless you chose the "without memories" link, its memories with their photos (at full resolution, including the metadata embedded in them), comments and likes. Journal entries, people and encounters are never shown. Visitors can also ask for a memory to be translated (see section 5). Revoking the link in the trip's settings stops it working.

6. How long we keep it

  • Account and content: until you delete them or delete your account.
  • Deleted data in backups: we back up the database once a day and keep the last 30 backups, so deleted data can remain in a backup for up to 30 days.
  • Server logs: 30 days in our monitoring system. A limited local copy on the server is overwritten as new logs arrive.
  • Metrics: 30 days.
  • Email confirmation links: 24 hours.
  • Payment records: Stripe keeps its own records of your payments, as the law requires of it.

7. Your choices and rights

  • See and correct your data: everything you create can be viewed and edited in the app. Change your display name and password in Settings.
  • Take it with you: each trip can be exported as a .traxj file, as a ZIP file with its photos, or as GPX.
  • Delete it: Settings → Delete account permanently deletes your account, your trips, photos, memories, journal entries, people, comments and likes, and your Strava and Polarsteps connections, straight away. Deleting your account does not cancel a paid subscription at Stripe: cancel it first with Cancel subscription on your plan page.
  • Withdraw consent for a connected service at any time by disconnecting it.

Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to access, correct, delete or export your data, to restrict or object to how we use it, and to complain to a data protection authority, such as {{SUPERVISORY_AUTHORITY}}. To exercise any of these rights, write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.

8. Storage on your device

TraxJourney does not use cookies. The app keeps the following on your device, in your browser's local storage or your phone's app storage:

  • your sign-in token (valid for 7 days);
  • your preferences: theme, map display mode, panel width, whether you have seen the introduction, and the server address if you use a self-hosted server;
  • the last trip you opened and its selection and filters, and whether a poster is being prepared;
  • a random visitor identifier used when you open share links;
  • if you use encryption, your device's encryption key, in your platform's secure storage;
  • on Android and iOS, a cache of trip data and photo previews, so trips you have opened load faster.

Google's sign-in and Stripe's payment pages may set their own cookies when you use them.
